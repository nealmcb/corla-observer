        How this works
        • Follow the audit board as it reads each ballot. Contest by contest (the default; switch at the top, at any time): confirm each contest as it is read, in each of the two readings, or type what you heard instead, or mark it not heard clearly. Your record names the contest. Enter confirms and moves on; the Keys button lists every key. Each contest's entry is signed like any other and sent in small batches, so "to send" counts go up and down as you work.
        • Two taps (the other way, fewer taps): each ballot takes two taps for a match: when the first board member has read it and it matches the screen, tap First reading; the button becomes Confirmed match (✓✓). When the second member has read it and it still matches, tap Confirmed match: that records the match and moves to the next ballot. On a keyboard, Enter or the space bar does each step. The two taps keep a mis-tap from recording a match, and both are recorded with their times.
        • If you hear a different vote, or anything else, type what you heard in your own words and press Enter, before or after the first tap. That records a difference instead of a match. Typing anywhere goes to the box.
        • If the board reads a different ballot, type its imprinted ID in the box (or tap Different ballot…). The end of the ID is enough when only one ballot on this board ends that way, such as 46-5 for 102-46-5. If it is on this board's list, the page jumps to it so you can follow its votes; the ballots skipped stay marked not read yet for when the board reaches them, and after the jumped-to ballot the page goes back to the first one not yet done. If the ID is not on this board's list, your note is recorded and the page stays where it is.
        • Until the county's pull list arrives, the split of ballots among boards is not known exactly, so a ballot at either end of this board's list may be the next or previous board's: it is marked BOUNDARY. If this board doesn't pull it, tap Skipped: other board; it then counts as done here. A ballot marked POSSIBLE is drawn only if the sample count turns out above its low estimate; if the board doesn't read it, leave it as not read yet.
        • Tap an earlier ballot to change its entry (the change is recorded as a new entry), or a ballot marked not read yet to enter it now.
        • Every entry is signed by this device, kept on it and sent at once. "Queued" means it will be sent when the connection is back. The server answers each entry with a signed receipt; its short code (such as 412-3f9a1c: the entry's number in the public log and the start of its hash) appears next to each ballot.
        • Download my record saves every entry with its signature and receipt, so you, or anyone you give it to, can check later that the published log holds exactly what you entered: corla-observer check-record. Use the same browser on this device for the whole session; another browser or device gets its own key.
        • When the county has targeted ballots too (ballots observers chose, examined after the state's sample), the list at the top switches between the RLA sample and each Targeted list. Each list keeps your place and your entries apart; a targeted list is marked TARGETED (NOT RLA) on the screen and its printouts. A ballot on a targeted list that is also in the RLA sample says so: enter it on the RLA sample, where it counts for both.
        • Look up a ballot (in the same list at the top) is for a ballot the board pulls from a storage box at the observers' request: type its imprinted ID, and its expected votes come from the county's redacted CVR, to enter contest by contest. These are recorded apart from every list, as box-targeted observations.
        • Don't interrupt the audit board; observers note, they don't overrule.
